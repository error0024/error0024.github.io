We revisit the encoding step of Winternitz one-time signatures, the building block of XMSS and SPHINCS+. We prove a lower bound on the verification cost achievable for a fixed signature size, which holds for general encodings, including randomized and non-injective ones. We then propose encodings that map messages into the top layers of a larger hypercube, deliberately allowing collisions, and show that they reduce verification cost by 20% to 40% at the same security level and signature size.
